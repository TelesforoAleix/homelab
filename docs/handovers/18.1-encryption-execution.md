## 0. Governance note

### 0.1 Why a sub-phase, and why it runs first

ADR-037 executes a decision Phase 18 deferred, so the work belongs to Phase 18 rather than to the
first phase that happens to need it (ADR-045 §2). It runs before everything else because it is the
only pending item that needs the owner physically at the machine, because every phase that puts
knowledge or project content on the node waits on it, and because delay itself carries risk while
credentials sit on an unencrypted disk (ROADMAP sequencing note, item 1).

### 0.2 This phase is in both risk classes at once

`safe-changes-headless.md` and ADR-041 draw a line between two things, and this phase crosses both:

| Class | What here | Control |
|---|---|---|
| **Lockout-class** (ADR-041 §3) — recovery needs physical access | A reboot into a rescue medium; edits to `/etc/fstab` and `/etc/crypttab`, which the standard's §1 table names as boot-class; new units | Classify first; the recovery step — the console — known in advance and **already attached**; validators run; rollback typed before the change |
| **Needs-a-verified-backup class** (ADR-041 §4) — no physical access recovers from it | The shrink. An `lvreduce` below the filesystem's size destroys the filesystem, and no console brings it back | A backup **restored and verified the same day** (§7.4 step A2), the order in §7.3 enforced by the tool, and a recovery branch (§7.5) read before the reboot |

ADR-041's whole point is not to conflate these. The runbook marks which class each step is in.

### 0.3 Execution is at the console, not over SSH

A mounted ext4 filesystem cannot be shrunk. Root must be unmounted, which means the running system
must not be running: the shrink happens from a rescue boot, at a keyboard, with a monitor showing what
is happening. **Monitor and keyboard are reattached before the reboot**, not after — all six DRM
connectors report `disconnected` today (S1), and a rescue shell with no display is a rescue shell you
cannot read.

The rest of the phase — creating the volume, LUKS, the unit files, the unlock path — happens over SSH
from the MacBook once the node has booted normally. The rescue session is kept as short as the one
operation that needs it. The monitor stays attached until the power-cut test has passed, because a
boot that hangs waiting for a passphrase is visible only there.

### 0.4 What this document is

The contract the execution stage is held to. Every step in §7.4 has a verification and an abort
condition; the execution records the actual output against each. A step done differently is a
deviation and is written down as one (PROJECT.md §11), not absorbed.

### 0.5 The reader

Per `AGENTS.md`, the runbook is written for a technically curious non-sysadmin at a keyboard with the
machine open in front of them. Where a step's *order* matters, the brief says why in plain terms
before it says what to type. The mental model that carries the whole of §7: **the filesystem is the
contents, the logical volume is the box, the volume group is the shelf.** You make the contents
smaller before you make the box smaller. You never make the box smaller than the contents.

## 1. Purpose

ADR-032's gate says the node may hold no knowledge base, no project content and no private
repository. The target architecture says Home Lab is the always-running system that holds the
knowledge. ADR-037 resolved the contradiction on paper: an encrypted volume on the node, unlocked
over SSH after boot, with the gate discharged for that volume only.

This phase makes ADR-037 true on the machine. When it is done:

- the root logical volume is smaller, and a LUKS2 volume occupies part of the freed space;
- the volume opens with the passphrase and **has been shown to refuse without it**;
- the node boots unattended to a reachable state with the volume locked, and **has been shown to**,
  by pulling the plug;
- a service that depends on the volume, started while it is locked, refuses legibly and writes
  nothing anywhere;
- the credentials that stay on root are an **accepted, documented risk** (ADR-046), not an open item.

The gate then moves rather than disappears: root still may not hold private content (ADR-037 §7).

## 3. Learning objectives

By the end the owner should be able to explain:

1. The three layers — filesystem, logical volume, volume group — as contents, box and shelf, and
   therefore why the filesystem shrinks first and the volume grows first.
2. Why `lvreduce --resizefs` exists: what a tool that enforces an order protects against that a
   careful human does not.
3. What a LUKS2 header is, why a header backup is taken once and kept off the machine, and why a
   header plus an *old* passphrase still opens the volume.
4. Why the wrong-passphrase test is run **before** the volume holds anything, and why a refusal that
   has never been paired with a success proves nothing.
5. What `noauto` in `/etc/crypttab` and `/etc/fstab` does, and what a boot looks like without it —
   the exact lockout ADR-037 §2 was designed to avoid.
6. The difference between a systemd `Condition` and an `Assert`, and why "skipped, with the reason in
   the journal" is the right shape for *degraded-until-unlocked is normal*.
7. What the power-cut test proves and what it does not.
8. What the credentials left on root can and cannot do — specifically, that none of them can open
   the volume (ADR-046).

### 6.1 The console session — decided: close it, as the first physical act

Session 29 is an authenticated shell on `tty1`. `sudo` would ask for a password, but `aleix` is in the

away from it.** A console idle timeout (`TMOUT` for tty logins, or logind policy) is a hardening

### 6.3 A second keyslot — recommended: yes

One passphrase in one password manager is a single point of failure for everything the volume will
hold. LUKS2 has 32 keyslots. Add one with a long generated recovery passphrase, **test both slots open
the volume**, and store the recovery passphrase somewhere that does not share a failure mode with the
password manager — on paper, away from the node. Its job is surviving the loss of the password
manager, not the theft of the room. The header backup (C6) is taken *after* the slot is added.

### 6.4 `discard` through LUKS — recommended: yes

The SSD is SATA and supports TRIM. Without `discard` in the crypttab options (and
`--allow-discards` on a manual `open`), Ubuntu's weekly `fstrim.timer` cannot reach the volume's
blocks. Cost: an observer of the raw disk can see which blocks are unused, a leak ADR-037 §6's threat
model does not care about. Benefit: SSD wear and performance over years. Take it.

### 6.5 A lock-state line in the bot's `/status` — recommended: yes, small

ADR-037's *newly required* list includes *a notification that reports lock state*. The push
notification is Phase 12's. But the bot already reports disk, uptime and load, and
`os.path.ismount("/srv/homelab")` is one line, needs no subprocess (ADR-023 §7's rule holds) and no
new permission. With it, the power-cut test's "bot working" also tells the owner the one thing they
need to know. Without it, between this phase and Phase 12 a power cut produces a node that answers
`/status` cheerfully and says nothing about being locked. Do it; it does not pre-empt Phase 12.

### 6.6 Whether the probe unit stays installed — recommended: yes, disabled

`homelab-data-probe.service` is the reference implementation of the dependency pattern and the
fixture for the power-cut test. It has no `WantedBy=multi-user.target`, runs only by hand or via
`homelab-data.target`, and writes one timestamp file. Keep it until Phase 18.2's first real service
replaces it as the canary, then remove it and say so.

## 7. Implementation scope

### 7.1 The sizing decision: 64 GiB root · 128 GiB volume · 43.42 GiB left free

**The numbers.** The VG has 60,268 extents of 4 MiB. Root becomes **16,384 extents = 64 GiB**. The
data volume is **32,768 extents = 128 GiB**. That leaves **11,116 extents = 43.42 GiB unallocated**.

**Why root keeps 64 GiB.** Root uses 8.9 G today, of which 4.0 G is the swapfile. What must fit:

| On root | Today | Budgeted |
|---|---|---|
| OS, packages, tooling (Docker Engine, both AI CLIs, snaps) | ~4.9 G | 12 G |
| `/swap.img` — stays where it is; a swap LV would be an fstab change for no gain | 4.0 G | 4 G |
| journald — capped by default at min(10 % of the filesystem, 4 G) | small | 4 G |
| Docker on root (`/var/lib/docker`): images, build cache. **Data holding content goes in the volume** (§16) | 0 | 20 G |
| apt cache, retained kernels, snap revisions | ~1 G | 4 G |
| ext4 metadata and the 5 % reserved blocks | — | ~4 G |
| Headroom | — | ~16 G |
| | **8.9 G** | **64 GiB** |

That is seven times today's usage and more than double a generous three-year projection. Root has no
reason to grow: ADR-037 §5 puts everything that grows in the volume.

**Why the volume gets 128 GiB.** Today's content is about 30 MB of repositories. Phase 10's indexes
— even a hundred thousand documents with 1,536-dimension embeddings — are under a gigabyte; Phase 24's
research cache and any container data are a few more. 128 GiB is an order of magnitude beyond any
credible projection, and it costs nothing to hold.

**Why 43 GiB is deliberately left unallocated.** This is the part that matters.

Growing either volume later is an online operation: `lvextend`, then `resize2fs` (and `cryptsetup
resize` for the encrypted one) — mounted, no reboot, no console, minutes. **Shrinking either is this
phase again**: a rescue boot, a monitor, the dangerous step. Allocation is a one-way door in the
cheap direction. Every extent given to a side that turns out not to need it can only be recovered by
repeating the most dangerous operation the project has attempted. The reserve is the ability to have
been wrong, in either direction, at zero risk.

It buys one more thing: **LVM snapshots**. A snapshot needs free extents in the VG, and the project
has never had any — which is why no phase has ever snapshotted root before a risky change. With 43 GiB
free, Phase 13's hardening and future OS upgrades can. Phase 01 gave the LV 100 % of the VG and this
phase is the bill; the reserve is how the mistake is not repeated.

**Why not 50/50, or everything to the volume?** Because root at 115 GiB would be thirteen times its
usage with no path to give the excess back, and a volume holding every free extent recreates today's
situation one layer down.

### 7.3 The order, and the tool that enforces it

**The order is not negotiable:**

```
e2fsck -f  →  resize2fs (filesystem smaller, below the target)  →  lvreduce (box to the target)
           →  resize2fs (filesystem grows to fill the box)       →  e2fsck -f
```

The filesystem is the contents; the LV is the box. The step that destroys is a box made smaller than
the contents: `lvreduce` to a size below what the filesystem occupies cuts the end off the filesystem,
and neither `e2fsck` nor a console nor anything else recovers it. The "below the target, then fill"
shape exists because a human types two numbers and might mistype one; with the filesystem first
shrunk *past* the target there is a margin between the numbers, and the final `resize2fs` with no
size argument simply fills whatever the box is.

**Decision: `lvreduce --resizefs` is preferred, and is what the runbook executes.** It runs
`fsadm`, which checks the filesystem, runs `resize2fs` to the new size, and only then reduces the
LV — the order above, mechanically, from one command with one number. It refuses a mounted ext4. On
lvm2 ≥ 2.03.17 (the node and the ISO have 2.03.31), a bare `lvreduce` additionally checks the
filesystem's size and refuses to cut below it unless told to resize it — a backstop, **which the
runbook does not test**, because a safety net you test by jumping into it is one you may fall through.

Why the tool over the manual sequence, for this operator: the manual path has two numbers that must
agree, typed at a rescue prompt, on the most dangerous step the project has attempted. The tool has
one number. It computes the filesystem size from the LV size, so the two cannot disagree. Its failure
modes are safe: if `resize2fs` fails, the LV is untouched; if the LV step fails after the filesystem
shrank, the filesystem is simply smaller than its box, which is harmless. The manual sequence is in
the runbook as the **fallback** — and so the operator knows what the tool is doing when it does it.

One caveat on the two forms. `--resizefs` (`-r`) is the long-standing option and exists on every
lvm2 the operator might meet; newer lvm2 also offers `--fs resize`, which does the same job natively.
Either enforces the order. The runbook writes `--resizefs`.

### 7.4 The runbook

Every step has **Verify** (what the output must show) and **Abort** (what makes you stop, and what
stopping means). Steps are grouped: **A** from the MacBook before anything physical; **B** at the

#### A — From the MacBook, the same day, before anything physical

**A1. Privileged confirmation of §2.2.** Interactive `ssh -t homelab`, so `sudo` has a tty:

```bash

```

- **Verify:** `VFree 0`, `60268` extents, `Free 0`; `ubuntu-lv 235.42g`; `pv_free 0`; the directory
  holds `token` (`root:root 0600`) and three allowlists (`root:homelab-bot 0640`); `running`; no
  failed units; the six listeners the record shows, no more; session 29 present. Paste the output
  into the handover's evidence.
- **Abort:** `VFree ≠ 0`, an LV other than `ubuntu-lv`, a missing allowlist, a failed unit. The brief's

**A2. The restore gate. [backup-class]** Run a **fresh** backup and prove its restore, today:

```bash
scripts/macos/backup-node.sh              # prompts: node sudo password, age passphrase
scripts/macos/verify-node-backup.sh       # decrypts, checksums, extracts, compares 111+ files,
                                          # plants a one-byte corruption and requires its check to fire
```

The verifier covers the node-state archive only. The repository mirrors are checked by hand:

```bash
W="$(mktemp -d)"; B="/Volumes/SD Card/homelab-backup/$(date +%F)"
age -d -o "$W/repos.tar.gz" "$B/repos.tar.gz.age" && tar -xzf "$W/repos.tar.gz" -C "$W"
for m in "$W"/*.git; do printf '%-28s ' "$(basename "$m")"; git --git-dir="$m" fsck --no-dangling && echo ok; done
rm -rf "$W"
```

- **Verify:** verifier prints `PASS`, including *"checksum check fires on a one-byte corruption"*;
  the critical-file count is `12/12`; ownership lines show `0/0 0600` for the token and `0/982 0640`
  for the allowlists; all five mirrors print `ok`. Read §7.5 now — the recovery branch — and
  confirm the USB, the Phase 01 guide and `scripts/server/install-*.sh` are in hand.
- **Abort:** any `FAIL`, or the planted corruption **not** firing (then every `ok` above it is
  unsupported). Do not reboot a machine whose backup you cannot restore.
- **Honesty:** this proves the archive decrypts and extracts byte-for-byte with the right modes and
  owners. It does **not** prove a wiped machine rebuilt from it boots — the Phase 18 loose end. That
  proof needs a second disk or machine and is inherited by Phase 14 (§16). The recovery in §7.5 is
  *reinstall + extract*, and the reinstall half was performed once, in Phase 01.

```bash
dmsetup info ubuntu--vg-ubuntu--lv | grep 'Open count'
```

- **Verify:** `1 logical volume(s) in volume group "ubuntu-vg" now active`; `ubuntu-lv 235.42g`;
  `findmnt` prints **nothing**; `Open count: 0`.
- **Abort:** the LV is mounted or has an open count → something in the live environment holds it;
  do not shrink a filesystem anything has open. Unmount / find the holder first, or reboot the USB
  and retry.

**B5. First check. [backup-class begins]**

```bash
e2fsck -f /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv
echo "exit $?"
```

- **Verify:** the five passes complete; the summary `…/ubuntu-lv: N/M files (…), B/C blocks`; **exit
  `0`**. A dirty-journal replay or orphan-inode cleanup on the first run is routine — answer `y`,
  then **run it again and require exit `0`**.
- **Abort:** exit `4` (errors left uncorrected) or `8` (operational error), or errors that keep
  reappearing. **Do not shrink a filesystem that is not clean.** `reboot` into the installed system
  (it will boot; nothing has been changed) and investigate over SSH.

Then, to know the filesystem *can* shrink this far:

```bash
resize2fs -P /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv
dumpe2fs -h /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv 2>/dev/null | grep -E '^(Block count|Block size|Filesystem state)'
```

- **Verify:** *Estimated minimum size* in 4 KiB blocks × 4096 is far below 64 GiB (expect roughly
  10–15 GiB; anything under `15728640` blocks = 60 GiB is fine). State `clean`. Record the block
  count: expect **`61714432`** = 60,268 extents × 1,024 blocks — the filesystem fills the LV exactly
  today, which is the same inequality as B8 holding with equality *before* the change.
- **Abort:** minimum size above `15728640` blocks. The disk holds more than §2 says; the sizing in
  §7.1 is wrong; stop.

**B6. The shrink. [backup-class]** Type it, then **read it three times before Enter**:

```bash
lvreduce --resizefs -L 64G ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv
```

Check, in the typed line: `--resizefs` is present; `-L 64G` has **no minus sign** (`-L -64G` means
*shrink by* 64 G, not *to* 64 G); the `G` is present (bare `64` means megabytes — `resize2fs` would
refuse, but do not lean on that); the LV is `ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv`. Answer `y` to the confirmation.

- **Expect** lines to this effect, wording varying by version: the filesystem is being reduced to
  `64.00 GiB` using `resize2fs`; `Resizing the filesystem … to 16777216 (4k) blocks`; `The filesystem
  … is now 16777216 (4k) blocks long`; then `Size of logical volume ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv changed from
  235.42 GiB (60268 extents) to 64.00 GiB (16384 extents)`; `successfully resized`.
- **Time:** minutes to perhaps twenty; ext4 spreads files across the disk, so blocks beyond the 64 GiB
  mark must move. **Do not interrupt it. Do not power off.** A `resize2fs` stopped mid-move is a
  damaged filesystem.
- **Abort:** non-zero exit *before* any `resize2fs` progress → nothing changed; read the message
  (usually "run e2fsck -f first"), redo B5, retry. Non-zero exit *after* the filesystem shrank but the
  LV step failed → the filesystem is 64 GiB inside a 235 GiB box, which is **safe**; either run
  `lvreduce -L 64G ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv` alone (its size check will now pass) or leave it and reboot —
  a safe, un-shrunk machine. **Never type an `lvreduce` whose size is smaller than what `dumpe2fs`
  says the filesystem occupies.**

**Fallback — only if `--resizefs` is unavailable or refuses for a reason you understand.** The
same order, by hand, with the margin:

```bash
resize2fs /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv 60G          # contents to 60 GiB: "now 15728640 (4k) blocks long"
lvreduce -L 64G ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv             # box to 64 GiB: "… to 64.00 GiB (16384 extents)"
resize2fs /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv              # contents fill the box: "now 16777216 (4k) blocks long"
```

The second line is the dangerous one, and it is safe **only because the first line ran and printed
`15728640`**. If the first line did not succeed, do not type the second.

**B7. Second check.**

```bash
e2fsck -f /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv;  echo "exit $?"
```

- **Verify:** exit `0`, summary shows `…/16777216 blocks`.
- **Abort:** exit `4` or `8` → the filesystem is damaged. **Do not boot into it.** This is §7.5.

**B8. The invariant, by arithmetic.** The whole phase turns on one inequality: **filesystem bytes ≤
LV bytes.**

```bash
blockdev --getsize64 /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv                                  # LV bytes
dumpe2fs -h /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv 2>/dev/null | grep -E '^Block (count|size)' # fs blocks × size
lvs -o lv_name,lv_size ubuntu-vg;  vgs -o vg_free,vg_free_count ubuntu-vg
```

- **Verify:** `68719476736`; `Block count: 16777216`, `Block size: 4096` → 16777216 × 4096 =
  68719476736 **≤** 68719476736 ✓; `ubuntu-lv 64.00g`; `VFree 171.42g`, `43884` free extents.
- **Abort:** block count × block size **greater than** LV bytes. The filesystem extends past its
  box. **Do not reboot into it** — §7.5. (Not reachable through `--resizefs`; the check exists because
  the consequence of skipping it is unrecoverable.)

**B9. Read the root before trusting it.**

```bash
mount -o ro /dev/ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv /mnt
cat /mnt/etc/hostname;  ls /mnt/etc/homelab-telegram-bot;  ls -la /mnt/swap.img;  ls /mnt/home/aleix
umount /mnt
```

- **Verify:** `homelab`; four files; a 4 GiB swapfile; the home directory.
- **Abort:** mount fails or content is missing → §7.5.

**B10. Reboot into the installed system. [lockout-class ends when SSH answers]** `reboot`; pull
the USB when the screen goes dark. Watch the console: GRUB, then Ubuntu, then the login prompt. **Do
not log in.** From the Mac:

```bash
until ssh -o BatchMode=yes -o ConnectTimeout=5 homelab true; do sleep 5; done; date
ssh homelab 'findmnt -no FSTYPE,SIZE,USED,AVAIL /; swapon --show; systemctl is-system-running; systemctl --failed'
```

- **Verify:** SSH within ~90 s; `ext4 ~63G 8.9G ~50G`; `/swap.img … 4G` active; `running`; no
  failed units; the bot answers `/status`.
- **Abort:** the boot stops at an error on the console → §7.5. A `swap.img` that fails to activate is
  **not** an abort: `swapon /swap.img` by hand, and if the file is unusable, recreate it
  (`fallocate`/`mkswap`) — its content is worthless.

**Leave the monitor and keyboard attached until D is complete.**

#### C — Over SSH from the MacBook. Two sessions; nothing here touches root's layout

**C1. Create the data LV.** The volume group now has 43,884 free extents; this step takes 32,768 of
them and leaves 11,116 as the reserve.

```bash
sudo lvcreate -L 128G -n data ubuntu-vg
sudo lvs -o lv_name,lv_size ubuntu-vg;  sudo vgs -o vg_free,vg_free_count ubuntu-vg
```

- **Verify:** `data 128.00g` alongside `ubuntu-lv 64.00g`; `VFree 43.42g`; `11116` free extents.
  `-L 128G` is absolute — no minus sign.
- **Abort:** *insufficient free space* → B6 did not land as §7.1 expects; stop and re-read `vgs`.
- **Declined by the orchestrator, 2026-09-12: discarding the freed extents first.** S2 proposed
  clearing the plaintext residue `resize2fs` leaves behind (it moves blocks off the end, it does not
  erase the originals) via a scratch LV and `blkdiscard`. The proposal was careful — it never named
  `ubuntu-lv`. It is declined because it is **incoherent with ADR-046**, not because it is dangerous:
  that residue is root's own data, root stays unencrypted by accepted decision, and the same bytes
  remain readable on the live root either way. Erasing the copy while leaving the original is
  ceremony. If the reserve is ever allocated to something that *does* need a clean start, discard it
  then, in that phase, with a reason.

**C2. Format LUKS2.** The volume is empty; this step is not dangerous **if the device is right**.

```bash
sudo cryptsetup luksFormat --type luks2 --label homelab-data /dev/ubuntu-vg/data
```

Type `YES` (capitals) when asked; enter the passphrase twice from the password manager.

- **Verify:** `sudo cryptsetup luksDump /dev/ubuntu-vg/data` → `Version: 2`, one keyslot, PBKDF
  `argon2id`, cipher `aes-xts-plain64`, 512-bit key; `lsblk -o NAME,FSTYPE,SIZE` shows
  `ubuntu--vg-data crypto_LUKS 128G`.
- **Abort — the one that matters:** if `cryptsetup` warns that the device *"already contains a
  'ext4' superblock signature"* or any existing signature, **you have named the wrong device**. A
  fresh LV carries no signature. Answer anything but `YES`. The only ext4 on this VG is root.

**C3. Open it.**

```bash
sudo cryptsetup open --allow-discards /dev/ubuntu-vg/data homelab-data
sudo cryptsetup status homelab-data
```

- **Verify:** `/dev/mapper/homelab-data is active`, `type: LUKS2`, device `/dev/mapper/ubuntu--vg-data`,
  `flags: discards`.

**C4. The refusal, proved — before the volume holds anything.** Close it, then run **the same
command twice**: once with a wrong passphrase, once with the right one.

```bash
sudo cryptsetup close homelab-data
sudo cryptsetup open --allow-discards /dev/ubuntu-vg/data homelab-data;  echo "exit $?"   # WRONG passphrase, deliberately
sudo cryptsetup open --allow-discards /dev/ubuntu-vg/data homelab-data;  echo "exit $?"   # correct passphrase
sudo cryptsetup status homelab-data
```

- **Verify:** first run → `No key available with this passphrase.`, `exit 2`, no mapping created;
  second run → `exit 0`, status `active`. Both outputs go in the handover, side by side.
- **Abort:** the correct passphrase is refused → you do not hold the passphrase you think you hold.
  **Stop before anything is written.** This is why the test is here: at this point a mistake costs a
  `luksFormat` again, not data. A wrong passphrase that *opens* the volume is not possible; if it
  appears to, stop everything and re-read what you typed.

**C5. Second keyslot (§6.3).** Generate a long recovery passphrase in the password manager (or
`openssl rand -base64 32` on the Mac) and write it on paper.

```bash
sudo cryptsetup luksAddKey /dev/ubuntu-vg/data          # asks for an existing passphrase, then the new one
sudo cryptsetup luksDump /dev/ubuntu-vg/data | grep -A1 '^Keyslots'
sudo cryptsetup close homelab-data
sudo cryptsetup open --allow-discards /dev/ubuntu-vg/data homelab-data   # with the RECOVERY passphrase
sudo cryptsetup close homelab-data
sudo cryptsetup open --allow-discards /dev/ubuntu-vg/data homelab-data   # with the PRIMARY passphrase
```

- **Verify:** keyslots `0` and `1`; both opens exit `0`. The refusal in C4 covers both slots — same
  mechanism — but each slot's *positive* control is run.
- **Abort:** the recovery passphrase does not open → `luksKillSlot` the new slot and redo; do not
  proceed with a recovery path that has not worked once.

**C6. Header backup, off the node.**

```bash
sudo cryptsetup luksHeaderBackup /dev/ubuntu-vg/data --header-backup-file /dev/shm/homelab-data-luks-header.img
sudo cryptsetup luksDump /dev/shm/homelab-data-luks-header.img | grep -E '^(Version|UUID)'
sudo chown "$USER" /dev/shm/homelab-data-luks-header.img
# on the Mac:  scp homelab:/dev/shm/homelab-data-luks-header.img . && age -p -o "/Volumes/SD Card/homelab-backup/$(date +%F)/homelab-data-luks-header.img.age" homelab-data-luks-header.img && rm homelab-data-luks-header.img
sudo rm /dev/shm/homelab-data-luks-header.img
```

- **Verify:** the dump reads as LUKS2 with the same UUID as the device; the `.age` file exists on the
  card; nothing remains in `/dev/shm` or on the Mac's disk.
- **Why:** a damaged header means the volume is gone *even with the passphrase*. The header already
  lives inside the LV; a copy on the same disk protects only against a partial overwrite. **The copy
  is secret material** in one specific sense: a header backup plus any passphrase that was valid *at
  the time of the backup* opens the volume even after that passphrase is later removed. Treat the
  card as Phase 18 already says to. Re-take the backup whenever a keyslot changes.

**C7. Filesystem.**

```bash
sudo mkfs.ext4 -L homelab-data /dev/mapper/homelab-data
sudo blkid /dev/mapper/homelab-data
```

- **Verify:** `TYPE="ext4" LABEL="homelab-data"`. The command names `/dev/mapper/homelab-data`; if
  the volume is closed the path does not exist and `mkfs` fails harmlessly.

**C8. The mountpoint, made unwritable underneath.** So that nothing — a mis-authored unit, a
careless `cp`, even root — can put content onto the *unencrypted* root filesystem at the path where
the volume is supposed to be (ADR-037 §7, mechanically).

```bash
sudo mkdir -p /srv/homelab
sudo chattr +i /srv/homelab;  lsattr -d /srv/homelab
sudo touch /srv/homelab/x;  echo "exit $?"
```

- **Verify:** `lsattr` shows `i`; `touch: cannot touch '/srv/homelab/x': Operation not permitted`,
  even as root. **The positive control is C10.**
- **Fallback:** if `mount` in C10 refuses to mount over an immutable directory (not expected), `sudo
  chattr -i /srv/homelab && sudo chmod 000 /srv/homelab` and record the deviation. Weaker — root can
  still write — but it stops every unprivileged path.

**C9. `/etc/fstab` and `/etc/crypttab`. [lockout-class — boot-class in its own right.]** This is
the second dangerous step, not a detail. Without `noauto` in **both** files, the next unattended boot
stops at a passphrase prompt nobody is there to answer — the exact lockout ADR-037 §2 was designed to
avoid, recreated one layer down. Two sessions. Back up first. Rollback typed in session 2 before the
edit:

```bash
# session 2, typed, NOT executed:
sudo cp -a /etc/fstab.pre-18.1 /etc/fstab && sudo cp -a /etc/crypttab.pre-18.1 /etc/crypttab && sudo systemctl daemon-reload
```

```bash
# session 1
sudo cp -a /etc/fstab /etc/fstab.pre-18.1;  sudo cp -a /etc/crypttab /etc/crypttab.pre-18.1
echo 'homelab-data  /dev/ubuntu-vg/data  none  luks,noauto,discard' | sudo tee -a /etc/crypttab
echo '/dev/mapper/homelab-data  /srv/homelab  ext4  noauto,nofail,x-systemd.device-timeout=10s  0  2' | sudo tee -a /etc/fstab
sudo findmnt --verify                      # with the volume OPEN (C5 left it open), so the source exists
sudo systemctl daemon-reload
systemctl cat systemd-cryptsetup@homelab-data.service >/dev/null && echo "cryptsetup unit generated"
systemctl cat srv-homelab.mount | grep -E 'What|Where|Options'
systemctl list-dependencies cryptsetup.target | grep -c homelab-data
systemctl list-dependencies local-fs.target  | grep -c srv-homelab
```

What each option does, because the operator must be able to read these two lines later:

| Option | File | Job |
|---|---|---|
| `noauto` | crypttab | The device is not added to `cryptsetup.target`: **boot does not try to unlock it and does not prompt.** Without this, boot asks for a passphrase on the console |
| `noauto` | fstab | The mount is not added to `local-fs.target`: **boot does not try to mount it** — which would otherwise pull the cryptsetup unit in anyway, `noauto` in crypttab notwithstanding. *Both files need it.* |
| `nofail` | fstab | If some future unit does pull the mount in, a missing device does not fail the boot. Belt to `noauto`'s braces |
| `x-systemd.device-timeout=10s` | fstab | If something starts the mount while locked, it fails in ten seconds with *"Timed out waiting for device"* rather than the default ninety |
| `discard` | crypttab | §6.4 |

- **Verify:** `findmnt --verify` → `0 parse errors, 0 errors` (a warning about the source is expected
  only if the volume is closed); the cryptsetup unit **exists** (so `noauto` is what keeps it out of
  boot, not a broken entry — that is the positive control for this refusal); `srv-homelab.mount` shows
  the options; **both `grep -c` print `0`**.
- **Abort:** any non-zero count, or a `findmnt --verify` error → execute the rollback in session 2,
  `daemon-reload`, and re-check before trying again. Do **not** reboot with either count non-zero.
- **Not done:** `update-initramfs`. Root is unaffected and Ubuntu's initramfs hook includes only
  crypttab entries needed to boot. A `noauto` data volume is not one.

**C10. Mount, and the positive controls for C8.**

```bash
sudo mount /srv/homelab
findmnt /srv/homelab;  df -h /srv/homelab
sudo touch /srv/homelab/x && sudo rm /srv/homelab/x && echo "write on the volume: ok"
```

- **Verify:** `ext4` on `/dev/mapper/homelab-data`, about 125 G; the write succeeds — the same
  `touch` that C8 refused, now landing on the volume.
- **Abort:** `mount` refuses because of the immutable directory → C8's fallback, then retry.

**C11. The target, the probe, and the pattern every later service inherits.** Two unit files.

**They do not exist yet — you write them first, in the repository on the MacBook**, as
`config/systemd/homelab-data.target` and `config/systemd/homelab-data-probe.service`, from the blocks
below. The node holds no repository (ADR-038 changes that only at Phase 18.2), so they reach it the
way every other config file has since Phase 03 — `scp` to `/tmp`, then `install` on the node:

```bash
# on the MacBook, after committing the two files
scp config/systemd/homelab-data.target config/systemd/homelab-data-probe.service homelab:/tmp/

# on the node
sudo install -m 644 -o root -g root /tmp/homelab-data.target /etc/systemd/system/
sudo install -m 644 -o root -g root /tmp/homelab-data-probe.service /etc/systemd/system/
```

`srv-homelab.mount`, named in the target's `Requires=`, is **not** a file you write: systemd's fstab
generator creates it from the `/srv/homelab` line added in C9. If C9 has not run, this target cannot
start, and that is the correct ordering, not a fault.

The contents:

```ini
# homelab-data.target
[Unit]
Description=Encrypted data volume unlocked and mounted at /srv/homelab (ADR-037)
ConditionPathIsMountPoint=/srv/homelab
Requires=srv-homelab.mount
After=srv-homelab.mount
# No [Install] section, on purpose: nothing pulls this in at boot. The unlock script starts it.
```

```ini
# homelab-data-probe.service  — the reference implementation and the test fixture
[Unit]
Description=Probe: a service that depends on the data volume (Phase 18.1 fixture)
ConditionPathIsMountPoint=/srv/homelab
After=homelab-data.target
PartOf=homelab-data.target
[Service]
Type=oneshot
RemainAfterExit=yes
ExecStart=/bin/sh -c 'date -Is > /srv/homelab/.probe && echo "probe: wrote /srv/homelab/.probe"'
ExecStop=/bin/rm -f /srv/homelab/.probe
[Install]
WantedBy=homelab-data.target
```

**The pattern:** `ConditionPathIsMountPoint=/srv/homelab` on every unit that reads or writes the
volume; `PartOf=homelab-data.target` so locking stops it; `WantedBy=homelab-data.target` so unlocking
starts it; `After=`. **`Condition`, not `Assert`, deliberately:** a failed condition *skips* the unit
— `inactive`, with the reason in `systemctl status` and one journal line naming the condition. It does
not fail it. So `systemctl --failed` stays empty and `is-system-running` stays `running` while locked,
which is what ADR-037 §4 means by *normal operating state* and what PROJECT.md §12 requires. An
`Assert` would fail the unit and mark the whole system degraded for what is, by decision, not a fault.

One consequence to know: `systemctl start` of a skipped unit **returns 0**. Anything scripted checks
`systemctl is-active`, never the exit code of `start`.

```bash
systemd-analyze verify /etc/systemd/system/homelab-data.target /etc/systemd/system/homelab-data-probe.service
sudo systemctl daemon-reload;  sudo systemctl enable homelab-data-probe.service
systemctl list-dependencies multi-user.target | grep -c homelab-data      # must be 0
```

- **Verify:** `verify` prints nothing about these two units (warnings from unrelated units in the
  dependency closure are the Phase 02 lesson, not a problem here); `enable` creates
  `homelab-data.target.wants/homelab-data-probe.service`; the `grep -c` is `0`.
- **Abort:** the probe appears anywhere under `multi-user.target` → a `WantedBy` is wrong; fix
  before any reboot.

**C12. The operator script.** `scripts/server/data-volume.sh`, installed to `/usr/local/sbin/`, three
verbs, each printing what it did and ending with `status`:

- `unlock` — `cryptsetup open --allow-discards` (prompts; the refusal proved in C4 is this exact
  command), `mount /srv/homelab`, `systemctl start homelab-data.target`.
- `lock` — `systemctl stop homelab-data.target` (which stops every `PartOf=` unit), `umount
  /srv/homelab`, `cryptsetup close homelab-data`. In that order: services, then mount, then key.
- `status` — is `/dev/mapper/homelab-data` present; `findmnt /srv/homelab`; `systemctl is-active
  homelab-data.target`; every unit wanted by the target and its state. **Works unprivileged**, so the
  owner and, later, Phase 12's watchdog can ask without `sudo`.

It carries a rationale header in the house style of `backup-node.sh`, and it is deliberately small.
Using the systemd unit (`systemctl start systemd-cryptsetup@homelab-data.service`) for the unlock is
equivalent and is noted in the script; `cryptsetup open` is chosen because it is the command whose
refusal the owner has already watched.

**C13. Lock and unlock over Tailscale, with the probe's refusal and control.** From the Mac, over
the `homelab` (tailnet) route:

```bash
sudo data-volume.sh lock
data-volume.sh status                                     # locked: no mapper, no mount, target inactive
sudo ls -la /srv/homelab                                  # empty, immutable
sudo systemctl start homelab-data-probe.service;  echo "exit $?"
systemctl status homelab-data-probe.service --no-pager | head -8
journalctl -u homelab-data-probe.service -b --no-pager | tail -2
test -e /srv/homelab/.probe && echo "LEAKED" || echo "nothing written on root"
systemctl is-system-running;  systemctl --failed
sudo data-volume.sh unlock                                # passphrase
systemctl is-active homelab-data.target homelab-data-probe.service;  cat /srv/homelab/.probe
```

- **Verify:** locked → `start` exits `0` **but** the unit is `inactive (dead)` with `Condition: start
  condition unmet`; the journal says `… was skipped because of an unmet condition check
  (ConditionPathIsMountPoint=/srv/homelab)`; `nothing written on root`; `running`, no failed units —
  **locked is not degraded.** Unlocked → both `active`; `.probe` holds today's timestamp.
- **Abort:** `.probe` exists on root while locked (`LEAKED`) → the pattern is broken; find out how
  before any real service is written to it. A failed unit while locked → the unit uses `Assert` or
  has a hard `Requires=` on the mount; fix.

#### D — The power-cut test (ADR-037 §4 and validation 6). Monitor attached; hands off

**D1. Precondition.** Volume unlocked and mounted; `homelab-data.target` and the probe `active`; the
bot answering `/status`; `date` noted.

**D2. Pull the mains lead from the M700.** Wait sixty seconds. Reconnect. **Touch nothing** — not the
keyboard, not the power button. The firmware's *After Power Loss: Power On* setting was validated in
Phase 01; the machine starts itself.

**D3. Observe from the MacBook.**

```bash
until ssh -o BatchMode=yes -o ConnectTimeout=5 homelab true; do sleep 5; done; date
ssh homelab                                  # then, on the node:
systemd-analyze;  systemctl is-system-running;  systemctl --failed
sudo journalctl -b --no-pager | grep -iE 'ask-password|homelab.{1,4}data|srv-homelab' || echo "boot did not touch the volume"
data-volume.sh status
ss -tln
```

Then, from the phone or the Mac, send the bot `/status`.

- **Verify:** reachable over Tailscale inside ~90 s; boot time within a few seconds of 27.9 s;
  `running`; no failed units; **the boot journal has no passphrase prompt and no mention of the
  volume, its mapper device or its mount** (`Reached target … Local Encrypted Volumes` is normal — that
  is `cryptsetup.target` itself, which is reached with nothing in it); status → locked, target
  `inactive`, probe `inactive`; the same six listeners; **the bot answers** — and, with §6.5, says the volume is
  locked. Nothing that lives in the volume is running (today that is the probe; §16 records that it
  stands in for the harness).
- **Abort / what the console is for:** if the console shows `A start job is running for …
  homelab-data …` or a passphrase prompt, `noauto` did not take. Boot will continue after the device
  timeout because of `nofail`; then fix C9 over SSH, `daemon-reload`, and repeat D. If boot does *not*
  continue, the console is the recovery: log in on tty1, execute the C9 rollback, reboot. **This is
  the lockout-class step's known-in-advance recovery**, and the reason the monitor is still plugged in.

**D4. Unlock over Tailscale.**

```bash
sudo data-volume.sh unlock
sudo dumpe2fs -h /dev/mapper/homelab-data 2>/dev/null | grep -i 'filesystem state'
systemctl is-active homelab-data.target homelab-data-probe.service;  cat /srv/homelab/.probe
```

- **Verify:** the passphrase is asked once; state `clean` (the abrupt loss was replayed from the
  journal by the pass-`2` fsck the mount unit runs first); both `active`; a fresh `.probe`.
- **Abort:** fsck errors on the volume → the volume holds nothing yet; `mkfs` again is acceptable,
  but record it, and repeat D.

A second run of D2–D4 costs two minutes and is recommended, not required.

#### E — Closing out

**E1.** `loginctl list-sessions` shows no `tty1` session. **Now** detach keyboard and monitor.
`/sys/class/drm/*/status` back to `disconnected`.

**E2.** A **third, fresh** SSH connection over Tailscale (standard §8). The LAN route stays filtered
by design.

**E3.** Against A5: `systemd-analyze security homelab-telegram-bot` still `1.3 OK`; `ss -tln` still
`6`; `id homelab-bot` byte-identical; `systemctl is-system-running` → `running`; `--failed` → none.
Record `lsblk -o NAME,FSTYPE,SIZE,TYPE,MOUNTPOINTS` and `sudo cryptsetup status homelab-data` — the
volume encrypted, nothing else changed (ADR-037 validation 7).

**E4.** `backup-node.sh` again — the node's state has changed — after `NODE_PATHS` gains the four
new files (§10). Then `verify-node-backup.sh`. Then close session 2.

### 7.5 If it goes wrong: the recovery branch

Read **before** B2, not during. Two situations, in increasing cost:

**The machine will not boot, root intact.** GRUB error, or a hang at a start job. The console shows
it. Boot the USB again, `vgchange -ay`, `e2fsck -f`, check B8's inequality, mount read-only and look.
If root is clean and the problem is a unit or fstab line, mount read-write, fix the file, reboot.

**Root is destroyed** — `e2fsck` cannot repair it, or B8's inequality fails after an `lvreduce`. There
is no repair. The recovery is **reinstall, then restore**:

1. The same USB installs Ubuntu 26.04.1 per `guide/01-ubuntu-server/` — done once before, on this
   machine. Choose the layout the installer offers, **but not 100 % of the VG this time**: leave the
   free extents §7.1 describes.
2. `age -d` the fresh archive from A2; `sudo tar -xzf node-state.tar.gz --numeric-owner -C /` per its
   MANIFEST. Netplan and the Wi-Fi passphrase come back with it, so the node reaches the network;
   `authorized_keys` comes back, so SSH works.
3. `scripts/server/install-tailscale.sh` and re-authenticate — the tailnet identity is deliberately
   not in the backup. `install-docker.sh`, `install-ai-clis.sh`, `install-telegram-bot.sh`,
   `install-model-helper.sh` — the account `homelab-bot` must come back as **uid 999 / gid 982**, or
   `SocketGroup=` and the allowlist modes break (`backup-node.sh` explains why).
4. Verify with `verify-install.sh`, `verify-remote-access.sh`, `verify-telegram-bot.sh`.

That path has never been executed end to end. It is the loose end Phase 18 named, and executing it
would be the proof Phase 14 needs. It is written here because the first time you read a recovery
procedure must not be while you need it.

### 7.6 Out of scope

Anything moving into the volume (Phase 18.2). The unlock notification and watchdog (Phase 12).
BIOS password, console idle timeout, TPM-bound credential encryption (Phase 13, via ADR-046). Root
encryption. A second disk. Bare-metal restore proof (Phase 14). Any change to the bot beyond §6.5.

## 8. Validation / tests

**An authorisation check that has only ever permitted is unvalidated, and so is one that has only
ever refused.** Every refusal below is paired with its positive control — the byte-identical case
with only the offending element changed, which must succeed. A refusal with no passing control proves
only that something is broken.

### 8.1 Planted refusals and their controls

| # | Refusal | Control that must pass | Step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `cryptsetup open` with a wrong passphrase → *No key available*, exit 2, no mapping | The same command, correct passphrase → exit 0, `active` | C4 |
| 2 | (same mechanism, second slot) | The recovery passphrase opens; the primary still opens | C5 |
| 3 | `touch /srv/homelab/x` as root while locked → *Operation not permitted* | The same `touch` after mount → succeeds, on the volume | C8 → C10 |
| 4 | `systemctl start homelab-data-probe` while locked → skipped, condition named in the journal, **no file on root**, no failed unit | The same `start` after unlock → `active`, `.probe` written | C13 |
| 5 | Boot does not unlock or mount: both `list-dependencies` counts are `0`; the boot journal has no `ask-password` and no mention of the volume, its device or its mount | The generated `systemd-cryptsetup@homelab-data.service` **exists** and can be started by hand — `noauto` is what keeps it out of boot, not a broken entry | C9, D3 |
| 6 | `verify-node-backup.sh`'s planted one-byte corruption is detected | The un-corrupted archive passes every check | A2 |
| 7 | After the power cut, nothing in the volume is running: target and probe `inactive` | After `unlock`, both `active` | D3 → D4 |

**Deliberately not tested:** `lvreduce`'s own refusal to cut below the filesystem size. The asymmetry
is total — a test that fails destroys the filesystem — and the order in §7.3 is the control, not the
backstop.

### 8.2 ADR-037's validation list, mapped

| ADR-037 | Where satisfied |
|---|---|
| 1. `findmnt` returns a shrinkable filesystem | §2, S1 — `ext4` |
| 2. Backup restored and verified immediately before | A2 |
| 3. `fsck` clean on the shrunk filesystem | B7 (and B5 before) |
| 4. Opens; fails on a wrong passphrase, by attempt | C4 |
| 5. A dependant started while locked refuses legibly, no partial start | C13 (refusal 4) |
| 6. Power-cut test: returns unattended, Tailscale, bot, AI not running, unlock over SSH | D |
| 7. `lsblk` / `cryptsetup status`: volume encrypted, nothing else changed | E3 |

### 8.3 Invariants checked throughout

- **fs bytes ≤ LV bytes** before rebooting (B8) — the one inequality the phase exists to respect.
- `systemctl is-system-running` → `running` and `--failed` → none **in the locked state**, not only
  the unlocked one. Locked is normal.
- `ss -tln` → 6 before and after. This phase adds no listener.
- `id homelab-bot` byte-identical; bot security `1.3 OK`.
- Nothing under `/srv/homelab` on the root filesystem, ever — checked while locked.
- Boot time recorded against 27.9 s whether or not it is favourable.

## 9. Security considerations

- **The passphrase never touches the node's disk, history or command line.** Only `cryptsetup`'s
  prompt. No `--key-file`, no `echo … |`, no environment variable, no `sudo -S` trick. **No key
  material for the volume may exist on root — ever.** No keyfile, no TPM enrolment, no cached
  passphrase. ADR-046's central claim — that nothing on root can open the volume — is exactly as true
  as this rule is kept.
- **The header backup** opens the volume with any passphrase valid when it was taken. It lives
  `age`-encrypted on the card, which Phase 18 already treats as secret material, and nowhere else.
- **The rescue USB is the USB-boot attack path** ADR-032 §3 named — used legitimately here. Anyone
  with this stick and the machine has root on it. That is the BIOS-password argument, unchanged,
  routed to Phase 13.
- **The idle console session** was a passwordless root-equivalent shell (§6.1). Closed; the rule
  going forward is log out before leaving.
- **Docker's data root stays on unencrypted root.** Images are public bytes and belong there;
  a container's *writable layer or named volume* holding knowledge or project content would violate
  ADR-037 §7. Phase 18.2 decides bind-mounts from `/srv/homelab` versus moving `data-root` — the
  latter makes Docker itself wait on unlock. Recorded in §16, decided there.
- **The shrink leaves plaintext residue** of root's former blocks in the freed extents (C1). It is
  root's data and exposes nothing root does not already; it is discarded anyway before the volume is
  created, so the encrypted volume does not sit on top of readable remnants of the filesystem it is
  meant to be separate from.
- **`chattr +i` on the mountpoint** enforces ADR-037 §7 for that path against root itself. It is a
  guard, not a boundary: nothing stops a unit writing content to `/var/lib/…` on root. Review is still
  the control; the immutable directory just catches the most likely accident.
- **The credentials on root are unchanged** by this phase — two OAuth files, the bot token, the Wi-Fi
  passphrase, and the Tailscale node identity. ADR-046 accepts that, with the reasoning, the loss
  procedure and the revisit triggers. This brief does not restate it; it points at it.
- **This repository is public.** LUKS UUIDs and `lsblk` output may be recorded. Passphrases,
  the header, and anything from the password manager may not. The scanners run before push.

## 16. Return handover requirements

The handover states, explicitly and in this order:

### 16.1 What Phase 12 inherits — by name

1. **The lock-state signal.** `data-volume.sh status` unprivileged, or directly `findmnt
   /srv/homelab` and `systemctl is-active homelab-data.target`. This is what the watchdog reports.
2. **The gap that exists until Phase 12 lands:** after a power cut the node comes back, the bot
   answers, and **nothing tells the owner the volume is locked** unless they ask (`/status` with §6.5
   says so when asked; nothing pushes). *"Back up, down 14 minutes, data volume still locked"* is
   Phase 12's first deliverable and the reason it runs next.
3. **The unlock procedure the reminder points to:** `ssh homelab`, `sudo data-volume.sh unlock`.
4. **The constraint:** the watchdog and notifier live on root and carry **no**
   `ConditionPathIsMountPoint`. They observe the volume; they must not depend on it, or a crash is
   silent (ROADMAP Phase 12). They also must not share a process with what they watch.
5. **What they may not hold:** no key material for the volume, per §9 and ADR-046.

### 16.2 What Phase 18.2 inherits — by name

1. **The volume as built:** `ubuntu-vg/data`, 128 GiB, LUKS2, mapper `homelab-data`, ext4 label
   `homelab-data`, mounted at `/srv/homelab`, root-owned and **empty** except the probe's file.
   Ownership and layout inside it are **undecided** — 18.2 decides which users own what.
2. **The reserve:** 43.42 GiB free in the VG. To grow the volume, online, no console:
   `lvextend -L +NG ubuntu-vg/data && cryptsetup resize homelab-data && resize2fs
   /dev/mapper/homelab-data`. To grow root: `lvextend -r -L +NG ubuntu-vg/ubuntu-lv`. **Never
   allocate the last extent** — the reserve is also the snapshot space.
3. **The service pattern** (C11): `ConditionPathIsMountPoint=/srv/homelab`,
   `PartOf=homelab-data.target`, `WantedBy=homelab-data.target`, `After=`. The probe is the reference
   implementation; 18.2's first real service replaces it as the canary and removes it, saying so.
   Scripts check `is-active`, not `start`'s exit code.
4. **Workbench must refuse to start while locked** (ADR-038's third validation) — the pattern above is
   how, and 18.2 proves it with Workbench itself, refusal and control.
5. **Docker's data root is on unencrypted root.** Any container holding knowledge or project content
   bind-mounts from `/srv/homelab`, or `data-root` moves into the volume and Docker gains the
   Condition — which makes Docker wait on unlock. **Decide, and record it as an ADR if `data-root`
   moves.**
6. **The mountpoint is immutable underneath.** Anything that wants to write must be on the mounted
   volume. Do not `chattr -i` it to make something work.
7. **Backup scope changes.** `backup-node.sh` covers node *state*, not the volume's *contents*.
   Repositories have remotes; `brain` is private and its remote is the backup — 18.2 says whether that
   is enough or whether the volume needs its own backup path.
8. **The header backup** must be re-taken if 18.2 changes a keyslot.
